## Verification notes for art

**Hammer price versus price realised.** This is the field's commonest error.
Most houses publish a "price realised" that INCLUDES the buyer's premium, which
runs to 25% or more on the first tranche. A hammer price and a premium-inclusive
price differ by roughly a quarter. Say which you are quoting, take both figures
from the same page, and never compare one house's hammer against another's
premium-inclusive total.

**Attribution is a value statement.** "By", "attributed to", "studio of",
"circle of", "follower of", "manner of" and "after" describe descending degrees
of certainty and enormous differences in price. Reproduce the catalogue's exact
wording. Never upgrade "attributed to Rembrandt" into "by Rembrandt".

**Editions.** A print, cast or photograph exists in an edition. Record the
edition size and the number of the individual work; a 1/10 and a 250/300 of the
same image are different objects at different prices.

**Withdrawn, bought in and guaranteed lots.** A lot that failed to sell has no
realised price, and an unsold lot is often listed without one rather than marked
as such. If a page shows no price, the lot did not sell — do not supply an
estimate in its place.
